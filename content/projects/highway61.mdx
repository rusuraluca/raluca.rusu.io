## Overview

Highway61 is an audio-first community for people over 50: topic-based chat rooms and weekly events where you can listen in, or talk when you're ready. During COVID-19 that meant an elderly person living alone could hear a friendly voice without wrestling with video-call software.

I worked on the React web app and the Express/Node backend, and helped move the service layer to gRPC microservices as the community grew.

## Why audio

The pandemic made isolation as painful as the lockdowns themselves. Most of our users' digital reference point was Facebook: familiar patterns, minimal steps, no assumption of technical confidence. Video meant camera anxiety and bandwidth problems; audio rooms meant showing up with a phone. Dial-in and mobile join weren't nice-to-haves, they were the product.

## What I built

- **Minimalist UI:** obvious labels, large actions, and a homepage where the next step ("join a discussion") isn't hidden behind menus
- **Topic-based rooms:** browse events and discussions, join when something resonates
- **Mobile join:** full participation using only a phone
- **Dial-in via Turbobridge:** phone-only users reach the same rooms as browser and mobile participants

## Architecture

As concurrent sessions grew, we moved to a **gRPC microservices architecture** so more rooms and more audio sessions didn't mean rewriting the core product.

- **Backend:** Express (Node.js)
- **Web app:** React
- **Realtime audio:** Agora SDK
- **Dial-in:** Turbobridge
- **Services:** gRPC microservices

## Outcome

Covered by [*The New York Times*](https://www.nytimes.com/2020/10/07/business/retirement/pandemic-life-seniors.html) in their pandemic coverage of seniors, and trusted by senior centers and aging agencies across the US to keep members connected remotely.

The design lesson I still carry: when your audience is non-technical, the interface should feel obvious on the first visit, and the hard engineering stays invisible behind it.
