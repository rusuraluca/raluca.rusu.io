## Overview

Highway61 is an **online chat community for seniors**. The platform is built mainly for people **over 50**, giving them a way to connect with others, learn new things, and stay socially engaged.

There are **weekly events and chat rooms on different topics** that users can join. During COVID-19, that mattered even more: many elderly people were at home alone, without family or friends nearby, and needed a low-friction way to hear a friendly voice and talk about something that interested them.

## Problem

During the first COVID-19 lockdowns, isolation became as painful as the lockdowns themselves. Many seniors lived alone and could not rely on in-person visits or complicated video tools.

The product had to work for people whose main digital reference point was something like **Facebook**: familiar patterns, minimal steps, and no assumption of technical confidence. Audio-only group rooms fit better than video: less camera anxiety, less bandwidth pressure, and more focus on conversation.

## Approach

We built Highway61 **from scratch** with room to **scale as the community grew**. The main audience was always users above 50, so every product decision started from accessibility and clarity:

- **Minimalist interface:** fast to understand, easy to use, even for people without technical knowledge
- **Topic-based rooms:** users browse events and discussions, then join when something resonates
- **Mobile join:** a flow that lets people participate using **only a mobile phone**
- **Dial-in access:** for users who cannot join from a browser, we added **dial-in functionality** through Turbobridge

Design borrowed cues from platforms seniors already knew: obvious labels, large actions, and a homepage that made the next step clear ("join a discussion") instead of hiding it behind settings or menus.

## Architecture

As usage grew, we helped implement a **gRPC architecture based on microservices** so the platform could handle more rooms, more concurrent audio sessions, and more users without rewriting the core product.

**Audio streaming** used the **Agora SDK**. **Dial-in** went through **Turbobridge**, so phone-only users could still reach the same discussions as browser and mobile participants.

**Tech stack:**

- **Backend:** Express (Node.js)
- **Web app:** React
- **Realtime audio:** Agora SDK
- **Dial-in:** Turbobridge
- **Services:** gRPC microservices

## Recognition

The work was highlighted by [*The New York Times*](https://www.nytimes.com/2020/10/07/business/retirement/pandemic-life-seniors.html) in coverage of how seniors adapted to pandemic isolation.

Highway61 was also trusted by senior centers and aging agencies across the US, including community programs that needed a proven way to keep members connected remotely.

## Outcome

Highway61 became a practical social layer for seniors: a place to join a room, listen in, and talk when ready. It reinforced a design principle I still use today: when the audience is non-technical, the interface should feel obvious on first visit, and the hard engineering should stay invisible behind it.
