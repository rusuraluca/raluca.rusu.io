## Problem

Missing-person searches often rely on photos taken years apart. A child’s school portrait may need to be matched against a sighting taken decades later, but most face recognition systems degrade badly as faces age.

## Approach

I built an end-to-end platform for aid workers and researchers: upload reference and candidate images, run inference through a trained vision model, and review matches in a web interface.

The stack was deliberately split by concern:

- **Python / Flask API:** model inference, preprocessing, and REST endpoints
- **Ruby on Rails app:** workflows, uploads, and result review
- **Docker:** reproducible local and deployment environments
- **Weights & Biases:** experiment tracking, hyperparameter tuning, and faster iteration loops

## Key decisions

Training focused on age-invariant embeddings rather than one-off accuracy on a single dataset split. Preprocessing pipelines were kept explicit and replayable so experiments stayed comparable as the model evolved.

Containerizing early avoided the classic “works on my laptop” problem when handing the thesis off for review.

## Outcome

The final model reached **94.61% accuracy** on the project’s evaluation setup. Experiment monitoring and structured preprocessing cut iteration time by roughly **40%** compared with ad-hoc notebook workflows.
