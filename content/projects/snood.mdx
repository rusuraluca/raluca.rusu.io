## Overview

SNOOD is a **full-stack web marketplace** engineered to tackle food waste by connecting local restaurants, bakeries, and stores with conscious consumers. The platform enables businesses to sell high-quality surplus meals at significant discounts at the end of the day, turning potential waste into additional revenue while offering affordable options to users.

## Problem & opportunity

**Environmental impact:** Over one-third of food goes to waste globally, contributing to roughly **10% of global greenhouse gas emissions**.

**Commercial waste:** About **40% of wasted food** comes from food service businesses like restaurants and supermarkets, often because daily demand is hard to predict and surplus is treated as unavoidable.

**User demand:** A case study of **400 local students** showed the gap on the consumer side:

- **75%** struggle to maintain a balanced diet on a budget
- **97%** regularly eat reheated meals
- **60%** are eager to pick up discounted orders in person

SNOOD sat at the intersection of those three forces: waste on the supply side, budget pressure on the demand side, and a pickup model students were already willing to use.

## Key features & solutions

**End-of-day surplus ordering:** Users browse discounted local meals, place orders through the mobile and web UI, and pick them up before closing time.

**B2B analytics dashboard:** Unlike many existing platforms at the time, SNOOD gave restaurant partners **monthly analytics** on daily waste and order trends, so they could adjust purchasing and cooking volumes over time instead of guessing.

**Tiered subscription model:** Flexible monetization for business partners, with **Free, Green, and Sustainable** plans that unlock deeper long-term waste reduction insights as partners grow with the platform.

## Achievements & scale

**1st place winner in the web section** (out of 30 competing teams) at **PoliHackathon 2022**.

The project was designed and built as an **end-to-end prototype** under hackathon time pressure, then grew into **direct partnerships with local businesses** and real customer orders.

It did not fully complete as a long-term product. SNOOD eventually lost ground to larger competitors already operating in the same space, including [bonapp.eco](https://bonapp.eco/) and [Munch](https://munch.eco/en/), who had more distribution, brand recognition, and resources to win the market.

## What I'd do differently

I would narrow the initial wedge even further: one city, one merchant category, one repeatable pickup flow, before expanding feature surface area or subscription tiers.
